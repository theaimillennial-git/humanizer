# AI writing tells: full catalogue

Common patterns in AI-generated text. These are signs, not proof: humans use all of them sometimes. Fix the underlying vagueness, not just the surface word.

## 1. Undue significance and legacy
AI inflates how important a subject is, in generic terms.
- Tells: "stands as a testament to", "plays a vital/significant/pivotal/crucial role", "marks a pivotal moment", "a key turning point", "underscores its importance", "leaves a lasting legacy", "continues to shape", "reflects broader trends", "solidifies its place", "an enduring symbol of", "deeply rooted in".
- Fix: state what actually happened or what the thing actually does. If the importance is real, show it with a fact. Otherwise delete.
- "The bridge stands as a testament to the region's engineering legacy." → "The bridge, finished in 1932, was the longest in the state until 1960."

## 2. Promotional language
Brochure tone, even in neutral content.
- Tells: breathtaking, stunning, nestled, vibrant, rich (cultural) heritage, picturesque, world-class, renowned, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, unparalleled, seamless, must-visit, hidden gem, boasts, showcases.
- Fix: replace adjectives with checkable facts, or drop them.

## 3. AI vocabulary
Words that spike in LLM output relative to human writing. One isn't a problem; clusters are.
- delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vital, intricate, multifaceted, nuanced (as filler), realm, landscape, navigate, journey, embark, foster, underscore, highlight (as verb, repeatedly), showcase, leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, streamline, robust, seamless, dynamic, vibrant, bustling, meticulous, comprehensive, holistic, synergy, paradigm, transformative, groundbreaking, innovative, game-changer, ever-evolving, ever-changing, in today's (fast-paced / digital) world, at the forefront, a myriad of, a plethora of, plays a role, key (as adjective, repeatedly), notable, noteworthy, enhance, encompass, garner, bolster, endeavor, commendable, invaluable, valuable insights, align with, resonate with, interplay, intricacies, navigate the complexities.
- Fix: plain words, or cut the sentence if the word was carrying an empty idea.

## 4. Superficial "-ing" analysis
A clause tacked onto the end of a sentence that adds interpretation without evidence.
- Tells: "..., highlighting the importance of...", "..., underscoring...", "..., reflecting...", "..., showcasing...", "..., contributing to...", "..., ensuring...", "..., emphasizing...", "..., symbolizing...".
- Fix: delete the clause, or turn it into its own sentence with a concrete, supported claim.

## 5. Negative parallelism
- Tells: "It's not just X, it's Y", "not only X but also Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y.", "More than just a...", "No X, no Y, just Z."
- Fix: say Y directly.

## 6. Rule of three
Triplets of adjectives, nouns or clauses used by reflex.
- Tells: "fast, reliable, and secure", "connect, engage, and grow", three bullet points every time, three examples every time.
- Fix: keep only what's real. Two items is fine. One strong item is often best.

## 7. Vague attribution and weasel words
- Tells: "experts say", "critics argue", "many believe", "studies show", "it is widely regarded", "observers note", "some have suggested", "has been described as".
- Fix: name the source, or state the claim as the writer's own, or cut it.

## 8. Hedging
- Tells: stacked qualifiers ("can potentially often help"), "may", "might", "could", "generally", "typically", "arguably" in every sentence; "it depends" without saying on what.
- Fix: one hedge where uncertainty is genuine. Commit otherwise.

## 9. Transitions and signposting
- Tells: moreover, furthermore, additionally, in addition, notably, importantly, consequently (stacked); "It's important to note", "It's worth noting/mentioning", "When it comes to", "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "Here's the thing", "The best part?".
- Fix: most can just be deleted. Good paragraphs connect through content, not connectors.

## 10. Section summaries and conclusions
- Tells: "In conclusion", "In summary", "Overall", "Ultimately", "To sum up", "At the end of the day", final paragraphs that restate the intro, a closing line about the future ("As X continues to evolve...", "The future looks bright").
- Fix: end on the last real point. If a conclusion is needed, make it add something (a decision, a next step).

## 11. Elegant variation
Cycling synonyms to avoid repeating a noun: "the city... the metropolis... the urban center".
- Fix: repeat the noun or use a pronoun. Readers don't mind repetition; they mind confusion.

## 12. False ranges
"From X to Y" where X and Y aren't ends of a real scale: "from small startups to global brands", "from ancient traditions to modern innovation".
- Fix: name the actual cases, or drop the range.

## 13. Formatting tells
- Title Case Headers. Use sentence case.
- Headers and bullets on short pieces that should be prose.
- Bolded phrases scattered through paragraphs.
- Bullets that are each a bolded label plus a colon plus a sentence, every time.
- Emojis as bullets or in formal text.
- Every section the same length; every paragraph three to four sentences.
- Fix: write prose by default; use structure only where it helps the reader scan.

## 14. Punctuation tells
- Em dash overuse, especially for asides and dramatic reveals. Keep one or two per piece at most.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes in contexts that otherwise use straight ones (mixed styles).
- Colons for fake suspense: "The result: ...", "The best part: ...".
- Rhetorical question then answer: "The secret? Consistency."

## 15. Communication meant for the user (chat leftovers)
Text pasted straight from a chatbot that still talks to the prompter.
- Tells: "Certainly!", "Great question!", "Here is a revised version:", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like...", "As an AI language model", "As of my last knowledge update", "[Insert X here]" placeholders, "Note: ..." disclaimers addressed to the requester.
- Fix: delete entirely.

## 16. Tone tells
- Relentless positivity; every problem framed as an opportunity.
- Unrequested moralising or balance ("However, it's important to consider both sides").
- Over-explaining the obvious.
- Uniform, polished register with no personality, opinion or specific detail.
- Fix: let the writer have a view. Include the concrete detail only they would know, if they've supplied it. Never invent one.
